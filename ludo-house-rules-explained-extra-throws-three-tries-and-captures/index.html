Ludo house rules, explained: extra throws, three tries and captures

Ahmed Moaz · 9 November 2026

Everyone's family has its own Ludo rules. Boardit lets the host choose, and every rule has a switch. This is what each does, and what is on by default.

The defaults

RuleDefault
Pieces per player4
Another throw after a capture or a piece homeOn
Three tries for a sixOff
CapturesOn
Time for each turn30 seconds

A six always throws again, whatever else is set.

Extra throws

Switched on, it gives another throw for a capture or for bringing a piece home. It keeps a good run going.

Three tries for a six

When no piece is out of base, you get three throws to find a six instead of one. Three throws give a 42.1% chance of at least one six, against 16.7% for a single throw. It helps when someone has been stuck.

Captures

You can turn captures off. Then pieces do not send each other home, and the game becomes a race. It is gentle, and it suits children who dislike having a piece taken.

Pieces

Quick Ludo gives each player two pieces and plays out in about five minutes. For contrast, the table log (13 September to 30 October 2026) puts the median four-piece game at 21.1 minutes.

A clock that bends

A host can pick a relaxed clock. It lets a player tap +30 seconds once a turn.

Which rules for which table

  1. Children: captures off, three tries on.
  2. A tight, competitive table: the defaults.
  3. A quick game: Quick Ludo with extra throws on.

You can set all of this at Boardit's Ludo.

One rule at a time

If you change three rules at once, you will not know which one made the game better. Switch one, play, and talk about it afterwards. The Ludo track has 52 squares, and a piece travels 56 steps to home, so every rule acts on the same path, and one change is enough to notice.

Steps, not squares

The full game and Quick Ludo differ by steps. A piece covers 56 steps between its entry square and home. With two pieces that is 112 steps for Quick Ludo, and with four it is 224. Set the extra throws against that number and the house rules look smaller.

Picking your own

Try the defaults first, then change one rule at a time.
