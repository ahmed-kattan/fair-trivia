Soccer trivia questions on clubs and grounds: ten from the Kop to the Bernabéu

Ahmed Moaz · 4 November 2026

In the United States the sport is soccer, and in most of the rest of the world it is football. People search for soccer trivia questions in either spelling, so I use both words freely below. These ten skip the World Cup and stay with clubs: famous grounds, rivalries and one very strange season. They are meant for a fan table, a pub quiz or a family that follows one team and argues about the rest.

Ten questions about clubs

  1. Which Spanish club plays at the Santiago Bernabéu stadium?
  2. Which English club's home ground, Anfield, has a famous stand called the Kop?
  3. Which Spanish club has the motto "Més que un club", "More than a club"?
  4. Which two Scottish clubs contest the Old Firm derby?
  5. Which Dutch club produced Johan Cruyff and won three European Cups in a row from 1971?
  6. Which Arsenal side stayed unbeaten for a whole Premier League season in 2003–04?
  7. Which club won the Premier League in 2016 after starting the season at odds of 5,000 to 1?
  8. In which city did Liverpool come back from 3–0 down against AC Milan in the 2005 Champions League final?
  9. Which Italian club is nicknamed "the Old Lady"?
  10. Which London stadium hosts England's home matches and the FA Cup final?

The answers, with a story for each

  1. Real Madrid. The ground is named after Santiago Bernabéu, who played for the club and then ran it for 35 years.
  2. Liverpool. The Kop is named after Spion Kop, a hill in a Boer War battle, and fans sing "You'll Never Walk Alone" before home games.
  3. Barcelona.
  4. Celtic and Rangers. Between them they have won over 100 Scottish league titles.
  5. Ajax. The club is famous for its youth academy.
  6. The Invincibles. They won 26 of their 38 games and drew the other 12.
  7. Leicester City. They had spent much of the previous season at the bottom of the table.
  8. Istanbul. Liverpool scored three goals in six minutes and then won on penalties.
  9. Juventus. Their black and white stripes were copied from Notts County in England.
  10. Wembley. Its arch is 133 metres high.

Which of these will split a table?

Questions 1 and 3 are easy for anyone who has watched Spanish football, since the two clubs have been rivals for over a century. The gentle trap is that both are giants, and a nervous player swaps them. If that happens, offer "Camp Nou" as a hint: it is Barcelona's home.

Questions 6 and 7 are the heart of the set, because both are about seasons that should not have happened. One side went a whole league campaign without losing. The other was a 5,000-to-1 shot and won the league. Read them one after the other and let the table argue which was the bigger surprise.

Question 8 is the hardest to place for anyone who did not watch it. Someone who knows the story may remember the score but not the city, so I treat the city as the real question.

How do you score a club quiz fairly?

Fans cheer for their own club and groan at the others, which is half the fun and a small risk. Agree before you start that a team name given with a nickname counts, so "the Gunners" is as good as Arsenal. Decide too whether "Man United" is enough for a full name.

Mix the order if one club has two questions in a row. Questions 1 and 3 both feature a Spanish giant, and the Spanish fan in the room will be unbearable if they come back to back.

When one club dominates the room

If half the table follows the same side, give each person a question about a rival. A Liverpool fan answering about Leicester, or a Real Madrid fan answering about Barcelona, makes a quieter and fairer evening than a free-for-all.
