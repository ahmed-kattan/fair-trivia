Bird trivia questions and answers: 10 for a quiz night with friends

Ahmed Moaz · 18 October 2026

Birds are everywhere, and almost everyone has a fact about one: the owl's head, the eagle's flag. That makes them a friendly subject for a quiz. Here are ten bird trivia questions and answers, from the familiar to the astonishing. Use them on paper, or as a round in a trivia quiz online with friends.

The questions

  1. Which bird is famous for fanning out colourful tail feathers?
  2. Which bird is the national symbol of the United States?
  3. Which bird is a symbol of wisdom and hunts at night?
  4. Which bird is the fastest when diving?
  5. Which Australian bird is famous for copying sounds, from car alarms to other birds?
  6. Which bird lays the largest eggs?
  7. Which flightless bird is the national bird of New Zealand?
  8. Which extinct flightless bird lived on Mauritius?
  9. Which bird has the largest wingspan of any flying bird?
  10. Which seabird has the longest migration, flying from pole to pole each year?

The answers, with a note on each

  1. The peacock. Only the male, which is called a peacock, has the big tail.
  2. The bald eagle. Benjamin Franklin once suggested that the turkey would make a better symbol.
  3. The owl. It can turn its head about 270 degrees.
  4. The peregrine falcon. A diving peregrine can reach over 300 kilometres an hour.
  5. The lyrebird. It has been recorded mimicking chainsaws and camera shutters.
  6. The ostrich. Its eggshell is strong enough for an adult to stand on.
  7. The kiwi. New Zealanders are often called Kiwis after the bird.
  8. The dodo. It was extinct by about 1681, less than a century after it was first seen.
  9. The wandering albatross. Its wingspan can be over 3.5 metres.
  10. The Arctic tern. It may fly about 70,000 kilometres a year.

How hard is it?

Questions 1 to 4 are comfortable for most adults. Questions 5 to 8 are medium. Questions 9 and 10 are hard, though a good guess on the albatross is within reach. If your group is young, drop the last three and add a question about penguins or flamingos from the easy end of the topic.

Why do birds make good quiz subjects?

Because everyone has seen one and few people have looked closely. A question about a bird people know, like the owl, can have an answer people do not, like the head turning. The gap between familiar and unknown is where quiz questions live.

How do you stage a bird round?

Make it a feature of a trivia night with a nature theme. Open with the bird set, move to sea creatures and close with a picture round of animals. A trivia game of three short rounds is a perfect length for a family evening. Print the trivia questions and answers with a gap for each team's guess.

How can you make a bird round louder?

Play recordings of bird calls between questions and let the table guess. Nobody will get them all, and the guesses will be the strongest part of the round. If you have a child who knows the birds in the garden, make them the guest judge for a question or two. Their pride is infectious.

A thought on the dodo

The dodo question is the one that gets a pause. It makes a point worth sharing: some animals vanished within a century of being noticed by people. It turns a quiz into a conversation.
