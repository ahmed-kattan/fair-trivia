Game night across three time zones: a planning guide

Ahmed Moaz · 12 November 2026

When your friends live in different cities, the hardest part of a game night is the clock. A game that suits one person's evening may be the middle of the night for another. These are the decisions I would make.

Find the overlap

Write down each person's free hours in their own time and convert them to one. Look for a window of at least an hour. If there is no overlap, split the group in two and play across two evenings.

Pick short games

Table log medians help here. For finished games between 13 September and 30 October 2026, the middle length was 4.3 minutes for Snakes and Ladders, 7.1 for Who's the Spy, 7.7 for Draw and Guess and 10.0 for Trivia Tavern. A game that long fits into a short window and leaves time to talk.

Avoid the long ones for a first try

Ludo had a median of 21.1 minutes and Property Rush 30.5, with about 50 for three or more players. They are fine when everyone has an evening, and risky when one person has to leave at nine.

Plan for a late arrival

Several games let a friend join one that has begun. A Draw and Guess latecomer guesses at once and draws later. A friend who arrives late can take over a bot's seat in Ludo, Snakes and Ladders or Property Rush. That means a table can start on time without waiting for the last person.

Use asynchronous games too

With Draw to a friend you send a drawing as a link and the friend gets three guesses whenever they open it. Finish a solo Trivia game or the Daily and you can hand a friend the same questions as a challenge. Neither needs both people online at once.

Agree on the call

A voice or video call on the side makes the time zones feel smaller. The games run in the browser, so each person only needs a link and a name.

A sample plan

  1. A ten-minute catch-up on the call.
  2. Who's the Spy, seven minutes.
  3. Trivia, ten minutes.
  4. A drawing round, eight minutes.
  5. Goodbyes.

How many games fit in an hour

Short games fit a short overlap. At the medians in the table log, an hour holds nearly 14 Snakes and Ladders games, 8 of Draw and Guess, 8 of Who's the Spy, 6 of Trivia Tavern, 3 of Ludo and 2 of Property Rush. Plan the overlap by the game, not by the evening.

The idea

Short games and a clear start travel across time zones better than a long one.
