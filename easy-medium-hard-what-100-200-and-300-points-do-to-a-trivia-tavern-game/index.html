Easy, medium, hard: what 100, 200 and 300 points do to a Trivia Tavern game

Ahmed Moaz · 11 November 2026

Every question in Trivia Tavern has a difficulty, and the difficulty sets how many points it is worth. Easy is 100, medium is 200 and hard is 300. The host can leave the difficulty on shuffle or fix it.

The default

The default is shuffle, a mix of all three. A round starts with easy questions and gets harder, and ends with a number question. The last round is worth double.

What fixed difficulty does

If the host picks easy, every question is worth 100. A round of ten questions has at most 1,000 points before any time penalty or order share. If the host picks hard, each question is worth 300, and a round of ten has at most 3,000.

These are ceilings. In the default race, only the first right answer gets the full amount, the second gets 75%, the third 60% and everyone after that 50%.

Why it matters

Points are only a scale. The relative score between players is what counts. A hard question worth 300 matters three times as much as an easy one worth 100, so a player who knows the hard answers can catch up.

Which setting for which table

  1. Mixed ages: shuffle or easy.
  2. A quiz-loving group: shuffle with hard questions in the mix.
  3. A first game: the default.

The double final

In the last round, everything is doubled. A table can turn a 500-point deficit around on a single good round.

A note on fairness

The questions are about well-known things and are not too hard. Even the hard ones are meant to be answerable by a person who follows the news, watches films and likes quizzes.

What a race pays on each tier

The three values interact with the race. On a 100-point question the race pays 100, 75, 60 and 50; on a 300-point one it pays 300, 225, 180 and 150. A hard question answered second is worth more than an easy one answered first.

The scale in a sentence

One hundred for easy, two for medium, three for hard, and double in the last round.
