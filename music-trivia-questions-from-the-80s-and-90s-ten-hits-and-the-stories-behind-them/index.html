Music trivia questions from the 80s and 90s: ten hits and the stories behind them

Ahmed Moaz · 30 October 2026

The 1980s and 1990s gave us charity singles, a clay head, a robotic voice and one famously argued-over word. These music trivia questions cover ten of those songs. Each answer comes with a short note, so the round still teaches something when nobody can name the singer. Read the questions aloud first, then the answers.

The ten questions

  1. Which singer had the 1980s hit "Girls Just Want to Have Fun"?
  2. Which singer was behind both "Careless Whisper" and "Faith"?
  3. Which singer had a hit with "What's Love Got to Do with It"?
  4. Which singer scored a 1990s hit with "Ironic"?
  5. Which singer's 1984 hit "Hello" came with a video about a clay head?
  6. Which 1985 charity song, sung by dozens of stars, raised money for famine relief in Ethiopia?
  7. Which rapper had the 1990 hit "U Can't Touch This"?
  8. Which singer's 1998 hit "Believe" made a robotic voice effect famous?
  9. Which band sang "Africa" in 1982?
  10. Which band recorded "Eye of the Tiger", the anthem of a boxing film?

The answers, with the story behind each

  1. Cyndi Lauper. She later wrote the music for the Broadway show Kinky Boots.
  2. George Michael, who first found fame as one half of the duo Wham!
  3. Tina Turner, who sold over 100 million records.
  4. Alanis Morissette. Many listeners like to point out that most of the things in the song are not actually ironic.
  5. Lionel Richie. He also co-wrote the charity song in question 6 with Michael Jackson.
  6. "We Are the World". The money went to famine relief in Ethiopia.
  7. MC Hammer. His baggy "Hammer pants" turned into a fashion craze.
  8. Cher. The effect was made with Auto-Tune and became known as the "Cher effect".
  9. Toto. An art installation in the Namib Desert plays "Africa" on a loop.
  10. Survivor. The song spent six weeks at number one in the US in 1982.

How hard is the round?

Three of the ten (the hits by Lauper, Michael and Turner) are medium: most adults know the tune, and fewer know the name. The "Ironic", "Hello" and charity-single questions are hard, because the title is famous and the artist is not. The "Africa" and "Eye of the Tiger" questions are easy the moment anyone hums the chorus.

A simple host trick is to hum the first line instead of reading the title. People who were not alive in 1985 still get the song from the tune.

Why do 80s and 90s hits work so well in a quiz?

I think it is because each song has a date, a story and a chorus that a whole room can sing. A date lets you ask "which decade". A story, such as a charity single or a studio effect, gives you the note that people repeat afterwards. A chorus gives the table something to do besides stay silent.

It also helps that several of these artists are famous for more than one song. If a table is stuck on "Faith", the clue "he was in Wham!" is fair. That is how I build the notes: one extra fact that helps the next person guess.

Can you play a round like this online with friends?

If your table is spread across towns or countries, playboardit.com lets everyone join the same trivia quiz from a phone or laptop, and you can pick the Music category before the first question. It is free, and guests only need a name. A table takes from 2 to 8 players.

Online trivia has one clear advantage for music rounds: no one has to pass a speaker around, because everyone sees the same four answers at once. In the default race the fastest right answer scores most, so a player who knows the chorus at once gets rewarded.

Planning a whole trivia night around the decades

One music round is a good start. For a full trivia night you could add a round for the 1970s, one for the 2000s and one for the 2010s, then end on a round of songs from the year the youngest person was born. Keep each round to ten questions and the evening stays under an hour.

Keep score on the notes

If you run this round at home with paper, give half a point to anyone who can repeat one fact from an answer note after the answer is read. It rewards listening, and it makes the round last a little longer without adding new questions.
