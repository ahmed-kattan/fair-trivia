Hosting a private drawing room for twelve

Ahmed Moaz · 13 October 2026

Twelve people can play a drawing game online in one room, and the game needs about half an hour. Draw and Guess seats up to twelve, and the shortest game it allows is two rounds, which means 24 drawings. Plan the evening around that number, not around the twenty minutes a table of six takes.

Here is the arithmetic, then a running order for a party with friends who each bring a phone, tablet or laptop.

How long twelve people need

Every player draws once per round, and hosts choose 2, 3, 5 or 8 rounds. At twelve seats the smallest game is 12 × 2 = 24 turns.

A turn has three parts: up to 12 seconds for the drawer to pick a word, the drawtime, and 6 seconds while the answer is shown. The drawtime options are 40, 60, 80 and 120 seconds.

DrawtimeLongest one turn can runCeiling for 24 turns
40 seconds58 secondsabout 23 minutes
60 seconds78 secondsabout 31 minutes
80 seconds98 secondsabout 39 minutes

These are ceilings, because a turn stops as soon as all the guessers have answered. The game page's own estimate for six players is 20 minutes for 18 turns, which is roughly 67 seconds a turn. Eleven guessers are less likely to all get a word, so I would budget near the ceiling. For a one-hour slot, pick 60 seconds and two rounds, and keep the spare time for the people who arrive late.

Before anyone arrives

  1. Set the table to 12 seats in the lobby and switch it to private. A private table is hidden from the open-tables list, and only people who hold the invite can join.
  2. Send the invite link to the group. Guests need no account and no download, only a name.
  3. Pick 60 seconds and two rounds. Leave the number of word choices and hints at their defaults the first time.
  4. Check the seat order in the lobby before you press start. It is the drawing order, and the host draws first in a room's first game.

Custom words suit a party, such as names from the group. The lobby asks for at least 10 to play on their own. Twenty-four drawings will want more than that, or you can blend the list into the standard one.

Running the night

Guessing happens in the game's own chat, so nobody needs to speak. That is the noise control. The one rule worth stating out loud is that nobody says a guess aloud, because the game hides right answers from the chat to protect the others, and a shout undoes that.

Turn order is the part twelve-person tables feel. You draw once, then wait through eleven other drawings. Tell the group that before the first word, and invite people to keep guessing quickly rather than hold back. A right answer never drops below a fifth of its speed points, so a late guess still scores.

For a second game, use the rematch. It moves the last player in the order to the front, so a different person opens.

If the room goes wrong

  • A latecomer: ask people to be seated before you press start, because the seat order is already fixed by then.
  • The drawer leaves mid-turn: the turn ends and the word is shown, and nobody loses points already earned.
  • The evening runs long: do not extend the rounds. Stop after the second round and take a vote on a rematch.

If your slot is only 20 minutes, split the group into two tables of six instead. Each table then runs about 20 minutes on the default settings, and both run at once.
