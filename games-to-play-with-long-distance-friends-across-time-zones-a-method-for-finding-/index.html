Games to play with long distance friends across time zones: a method for finding the shared hour

Ahmed Moaz · 30 October 2026

Picking games to play with long distance friends is the easy half. The hard half is the clock. When one of you is eating breakfast while the other is going to bed, "Thursday evening" means nothing. This article gives a method for finding the shared hour, then matches a game to however much time you have.

Every figure below is a worked example with invented windows, not a record of any group. Swap in your own times and the method still holds.

Why can't these games be played slowly?

Be honest about the format first. The games on my site are live free online games, and they need the whole table together. Ludo has a thirty-second clock on each turn, and Snakes and Ladders has the same. Property Rush gives sixty seconds, then a personal bank of three minutes to use across the match. Who's the Spy gives twenty-five seconds per clue turn. In the games that have computer-played seats, a person who misses two turns running is replaced until they return.

So these are not games you play by taking a turn each morning for a week. You need everyone together, and the question is how to find that time.

What is the method?

Write down, for each person, the hours they are free on a weekday and the hours they are free at a weekend. Convert everyone to one clock, for example the clock of the person who is hosting. Find the overlap. That is the whole method, and the sums are short.

What does the arithmetic show?

Suppose each friend is free from 18:00 to 22:00 on a weekday, which is a four-hour window. If the clocks differ by three hours, the windows overlap for one hour. If they differ by four hours or more, they do not overlap at all. So for a weekday game, a gap of more than three hours already breaks the plan.

Now suppose each friend is free from 10:00 to 23:00 on a weekend, a thirteen-hour window. The overlap is thirteen hours minus the gap.

  • A gap of 3 hours leaves 10 hours.
  • A gap of 6 hours leaves 7 hours.
  • A gap of 9 hours leaves 4 hours.
  • A gap of 12 hours leaves 1 hour.

The lesson is plain: beyond a few hours of difference, the weekend does the work. A friend's late morning is your evening, and that is the slot to protect.

Which game fits which hour?

Match the length of the game to the length of the overlap. If you cannot see each other, a video call on a second window is the simplest way to share the evening.

  • One hour of overlap: a quiz at about fourteen minutes and a drawing round, with a chat around them.
  • A short gap, such as when one person is at lunch and the other winding down for the evening: Quick Ludo, about five minutes, or Snakes and Ladders, which fits in anything from ten minutes to just under half an hour.
  • A long weekend window: a full Ludo game or a property trading game, both of which want a clear hour.

What about when you cannot overlap at all?

A few things keep the friendship alive without a shared hour. Trivia Tavern has a solo mode, and there is a daily set of five questions, so each of you can play alone and post a score in a shared chat. That cannot match a live table, but it keeps a contest going on the days when the clocks refuse to meet.

What changes twice a year?

If only one of your countries changes its clocks, the gap moves by an hour. A slot that worked in summer may be an hour late in winter. Look at the gap again when the clocks change, and tell the group a week before.

How do you make the most of that hour?

Make the table before the hour begins, so that nobody burns the overlap waiting. I keep these games on Boardit, where a guest only types a name and nothing is downloaded, which matters when you have one precious hour. Send the code in a private message, since anyone who holds it can sit down.

Start your game night on time, even if one person is late. A table that waits for stragglers uses up the same hour you worked to find.

Which person should pay the cost of the awkward hour?

Rotate it. If the only workable slot is a late night for one person, alternate so the night owl hosts one week and the early riser the next. Shared inconvenience feels fair, and fairness is what keeps a standing game going for months.

A short checklist

  1. List free hours for each person, weekday and weekend.
  2. Convert to one clock and subtract the gap.
  3. Choose a game no longer than the overlap.
  4. Recheck the gap when the clocks change.
