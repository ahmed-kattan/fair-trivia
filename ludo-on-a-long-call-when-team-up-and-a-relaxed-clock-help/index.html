Ludo on a long call: when Team-up and a relaxed clock help

Ahmed Moaz · 15 November 2026

A call changes how a board game feels. People talk more and watch less, so a game with a clear rhythm suits it. Ludo can work well, if the host chooses the settings for talking.

Team-up

Teams of two put partners opposite, and partners never capture each other. On a call, it gives each player someone to talk to about the move. A pair can plan together, while two other pairs sit in a different tile of the call.

Empty seats get bots, so you can play with only two friends against two bots. Once a player's pieces are home they keep throwing for their partner, so nobody has to watch.

The relaxed clock

Turns last 30 seconds by default. On a call, people are talking and may not notice it is their turn. A relaxed clock of 60 seconds is a host option, and each player may ask for 30 more seconds once during a turn.

How long it takes

Finished Ludo games in the log from 13 September to 30 October 2026 have a median of 21.1 minutes. A call with talk will run a little longer, and Quick Ludo with two pieces each takes about five minutes if you want to keep it short.

Tips

  1. Say your throw out loud. The call has no dice sound.
  2. Name your colour. Everyone should know who is who.
  3. Agree on captures. If a friend dislikes losing a piece, switch captures off.
  4. Use the stars. Say "safe" when someone reaches one.

When someone drops

A signal on every table is green, yellow or red. A failed connection is shown to the player, who can retry while the seat is held. A bot covers a player after two missed turns.

Where to play

Both settings are on Boardit's Ludo, for two to four players.

Eight throws and no six

Ludo has an unlucky streak that a long call makes worse. With one try for a six, 23.3% of players have still not thrown one after eight throws, and on a call where people drift off between turns that wait is when attention goes.

The call in a line

Teams to talk, a slow clock and a bot as a net.
