How many throws does it take to reach 100? Snakes and ladders game odds

Ahmed Moaz · 24 October 2026

Nobody who rolls the die in a snakes and ladders game knows how long the journey will be. But if you add up every possible game, a clear picture appears, and it is stranger than you would think. Here is the whole spread for one counter on the board I run.

What is the typical number of throws?

The average is 39.2 throws. The median, the point where half of games are shorter and half are longer, is 32. The two numbers differ because the distribution has a long tail: a snake near the end can undo a game that looked won, so a few games drag on far longer than the rest.

How wide is the spread?

Here are the markers I calculated for one counter:

  • Fastest possible game: 7 throws
  • Median: 32 throws
  • Mean: 39.2 throws
  • 90th percentile: 72 throws, so one game in ten takes longer than this
  • 99th percentile: 128 throws, so one game in a hundred takes longer

The gap between 7 and 128 is a factor of eighteen. If a classroom plays the same board, they will see games that end almost instantly and games that nobody finishes before the bell.

What are the odds of finishing by a given turn?

For a short game, the odds are lower than most people expect:

  • By throw 20: 22.3% of counters have finished
  • By throw 30: 45.9%
  • By throw 50: 75.9%

So after thirty throws, more than half of single counters are still going. After fifty, about a quarter are still on the board. When people feel that a game "keeps going on", this is why: the maths agrees.

Why is the finish so slow?

Two things slow the end. First, the exact-finish rule: a counter near the top must hit the last square exactly. Second, the snakes cluster in the top half of the board. Of the ten snake heads on my board, nine are on squares above 45, and three of them, at 93, 95 and 98, sit in the last ten squares. A counter that has done most of the climb still has to run through that.

What does this mean for planning?

If you are hosting, tell people what to expect. For a quick snakes and ladders online game that finishes in about a quarter of an hour, play with four or more people, because the first to finish ends everything. For a longer, calmer session, play with two.

A useful rule of thumb: add up about one minute for every three throws for a relaxed family, and keep an eye on the clock. For a family game night that has to end before bedtime, set a cap, such as stopping at the first player past square 90.

Where to try it

You can play the same board on Board It, my own site: a free multiplayer table for two players up to eight, with the printed snakes and ladders rules and no download. If you keep a tally of throws in a notebook for ten games, you will see your own spread, and it will look a lot like the one above.

A tip for teachers

Ask students to guess the average before they play, then reveal the figure. Guesses cluster around 20 or 25, because adults and children picture a hundred squares divided by the 3.5 an average throw gives you. The answer, 39, surprises them, and it opens a conversation about why snakes pull the average up.

How to read these numbers fairly

The figures describe a single counter, so they do not include waiting for other players. They also depend on the board layout: a board with fewer snakes would finish faster. Treat the shape, a short fastest game and a very long tail, as the general lesson.
