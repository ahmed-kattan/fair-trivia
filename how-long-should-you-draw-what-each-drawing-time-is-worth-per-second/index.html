How long should you draw? What each drawing time is worth per second

Ahmed Moaz · 11 November 2026

Draw and Guess lets the host pick the drawing time: 40, 60, 80, 100 or 120 seconds. The choice changes more than the length of a game. It changes what each second is worth.

The arithmetic

A guess is worth between 100 and 500 points. The range is 400 points, spread across the drawing time. So each second left is worth 400 divided by the time.

Drawing timePoints per second left
40 seconds10
60 seconds6.7
80 seconds5
100 seconds4
120 seconds3.3

What it means

At 40 seconds a second matters: a guess two seconds later costs 20 points. At 120 seconds it costs about 7. A short clock makes the scoring sharp, so a fast guess pays a lot. A long clock makes it gentle.

The length of a game

The longest a drawing can take is the clock. A table of eight playing three rounds is 24 drawings. At 40 seconds that is 16 minutes at most. At 120 seconds it is 48.

Which to choose

  1. 40 or 60 seconds: for a quick group and a competitive game.
  2. 80 or 100: for most tables. The default is 100.
  3. 120: for beginners, children, or a table that talks as it draws.

A thing I notice

The drawing is usually guessed before the clock runs out, so a long clock rarely makes a game longer. It makes the early seconds count a little less.

Where to try it

Boardit's Draw and Guess is free, for two to twelve players, and the host sets the drawing time in the lobby.

Using the table

If your group likes a tense game, pick 40 or 60 and watch how much a single second matters. If your group likes to chat, pick 100 or 120 and let the points slide gently. The scoring is the same shape either way. What changes is how fast a delay is punished, and so how serious the game feels.

A quick sketch at 75 seconds

The points are linear in the time left, which makes them easy to reason about. On the default 100-second clock a guess made with 75 seconds left is worth 400 points, and one with half the time left is worth 300, so each quarter of the clock is worth 100 points.

A rule of thumb

Shorter for sharper, longer for kinder.
