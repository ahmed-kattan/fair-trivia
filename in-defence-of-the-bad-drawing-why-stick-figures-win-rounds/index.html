In defence of the bad drawing: why stick figures win rounds

Ahmed Moaz · 12 October 2026

A stick figure the table reads in ten seconds scores more than a careful sketch that takes forty. In Draw and Guess, one of the drawing games online where points fall with the clock, that is arithmetic and not an opinion. Here is the arithmetic for an 80-second turn, the reason detail is slow, and the words where the stick figure loses.

The arithmetic of a slow masterpiece

A right guess scores 100 + 400 × (seconds left ÷ drawing time), rounded to the nearest 5. On an 80-second clock:

  • a guess with 70 seconds left is worth 450;
  • with 40 seconds left, 300;
  • with 10 seconds left, 150.

Now say the detailed version needs 30 more seconds before anyone can name it. The first guesser's 450 becomes 300. That is a third gone before anyone has misspelled anything.

The drawer feels it too, because the drawer's pay is built from the guessers' speed scores. Take a table of four with three right guesses. If the stick figure is read with 70, 65 and 60 seconds left, the speed points are 450, 425 and 400. They add up to 1,275, and divided among the three other players the drawer earns 425.

If the careful sketch is read 30 seconds later, the speeds are 300, 275 and 250. That adds up to 825, and the drawer earns 275. Same word, same table, 150 fewer points, and the only difference is how fast the drawing became readable.

A budget for 80 seconds

Think of the clock as a budget. With the default two hints, a letter appears at 53 and 27 seconds left. A new right guess is worth 365 at the first hint and 235 at the second, against 500 at the very start (100 + 400 × 53 ÷ 80, and 100 + 400 × 27 ÷ 80). So the pay for a late guess drops by more than a quarter before the first hint even lands.

That suggests a plan. Spend the first ten seconds on the one shape that says the word. Add a single distinguishing detail next. Stop before the first hint unless the chat is still wrong.

Why detail is slow

Guessers do not start from nothing. They see a row of dashes with the length of each word, so a drawing only has to narrow things down. The game also gives hints. With the default two hints on an 80-second clock, a letter appears at 53 seconds left and another at 27. A drawing that is still unreadable by then is racing the hints as well as the other guessers.

The drawer also has choices. The game offers a few words and the drawer has 12 seconds to choose, so the first skill is choosing the word that needs the fewest lines. That is a decision about scoring as much as about art.

Where the stick figure loses

Some words do not shrink. A word whose whole point is a fine detail, or an idea with no object behind it, gives a stick figure nothing to show. A drawer who gets only abstract choices has to draw more, and the clock still runs. I cannot say from data how often simple drawings are guessed faster than detailed ones at a real table. The claim here is about the score formula, which pays for time, not about how people read pictures.
