Thanksgiving trivia questions on its history: 10 from Plymouth to the turkey pardon

Ahmed Moaz · 27 October 2026

Thanksgiving trivia questions about history are the ones people argue over, because everyone learned a slightly different version at school. These ten start at Plymouth in 1621 and end with a bird that gets a presidential pardon. I wrote them myself and checked each answer on a named page. The last question asks for a number, and the nearest guess wins it.

The ten questions

  1. How many days did the Plymouth feast of 1621 last?
  2. The 1621 feast was not called a thanksgiving at the time. In 1841 a footnote gave it a name that stuck. What was it?
  3. Which governor of Plymouth planned a thanksgiving feast in 1623?
  4. Which president's 1863 proclamation set apart the last Thursday of November?
  5. Which writer, called the chief promoter of the modern holiday, wrote editorials urging that proclamation?
  6. In 1941 Congress and President Roosevelt fixed Thanksgiving on which Thursday of November?
  7. In which year was the first Macy's parade held in New York, and from which neighbourhood did it set off?
  8. Which president began the formal turkey pardon in 1989?
  9. In Canada, Thanksgiving falls on the second Monday of which month?
  10. According to Edward Winslow's account, about how many men came with the leader Massasoit to the 1621 feast?

Answers and where they come from

  1. Three days. Winslow wrote that the Pilgrims "entertained and feasted" their guests.
  2. "The first Thanksgiving." The name was applied later, so the 1621 gathering is better described as a harvest feast.
  3. William Bradford.
  4. Abraham Lincoln. The date in 1863 was 26 November.
  5. Sarah Josepha Hale. Lincoln's proclamation followed a series of her editorials.
  6. The fourth Thursday. Roosevelt signed the bill on 26 December 1941.
  7. 1924, from 145th Street in Harlem, ending at Macy's store at Herald Square. The route has changed since.
  8. George H. W. Bush. Ronald Reagan joked about a "pardon" in 1987, but the formal ritual began with Bush.
  9. October.
  10. About ninety.

Which of these catch people out?

Number 2 is the one I expect to start an argument, because the 1621 meal was a harvest feast and the label "first Thanksgiving" came much later. Question 6 also surprises people who assume the date was always set by Lincoln. His order named the last Thursday, and the fourth Thursday came from the 1941 law.

For a mixed table, keep questions 4, 8 and 9 for the start. Most adults can answer them without a prompt.

How do you run the ten at a table?

Print the list once and keep a second copy with the answers for the person reading. Read one question, give people twenty seconds, then ask for answers together. A point each is enough. If two teams tie, question 10 is your tie-break, since it asks for a number.

A short Thanksgiving trivia night can follow a simple shape. Start with a general knowledge round about food and family. Move to these ten as the history round. Finish with something light, such as a film or music round, so the table leaves laughing and not arguing about Bradford.

Keep a record of who answered what. Next year you can write a new set and see who improved. Every answer here comes from the Wikipedia pages on Thanksgiving, the Macy's parade or the turkey pardon, so a guest who disagrees can look it up and read it.

A note on the first feast

The 1621 meal is known from a few short accounts, so I would be careful with any detail beyond the ones above. When a guest says the Pilgrims ate a particular dish, ask where they read it. That question is more useful at the table than any date.
