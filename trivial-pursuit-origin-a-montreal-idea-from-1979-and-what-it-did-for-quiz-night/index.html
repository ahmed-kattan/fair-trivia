Trivial Pursuit origin: a Montreal idea from 1979 and what it did for quiz night

Ahmed Moaz · 18 October 2026

Trivial Pursuit is the board game that made quizzing a living-room pastime. The Trivial Pursuit origin story is short enough to tell in one sitting, and it holds lessons for anyone writing a quiz today. This account follows the Wikipedia article "Trivial Pursuit" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivial_Pursuit), and ends with how to play a quiz online on a free site I make, Boardit.

Where and when was it created?

The game was created on 15 December 1979 in Montreal, Canada. That is the date the sources give for the idea. The game itself was released in 1981, so there is a gap of about two years between the idea and the shop shelf.

How successful did it become?

Very. The reference article says more than 100 million copies had been sold by 2014. That makes it one of the strongest known quiz games ever sold, and it is a large reason why trivia nights feel familiar to most people.

What did the game change?

It turned quiz knowledge into a board game with a board, a token and a goal. Players move round the board and collect wedges by answering questions in categories. That structure, with categories and a visible goal, is now the model for almost every quiz game.

What can a quiz writer learn from it?

  • Categories give shape. A quiz with themes feels like a journey, not a pile of questions.
  • Progress should be visible. Collecting a wedge is a small reward you can see.
  • Questions should be answerable. A good question can be answered by someone who knows, and checked afterwards.
  • A mix of difficulty keeps everyone in. Easy ones draw players in, hard ones give the experts a moment.

How does the online quiz differ?

A paper board is replaced by a screen, and the answer cards by multiple-choice buttons. The game still has categories, but the scoring is automatic. The host no longer reads cards aloud, because each player sees the question on their own phone.

Can you play it with a group on a call?

Yes. Share a link and sit at the same table. Choose a topic, answer on your own device, and watch the scores update. The quiz on Boardit seats two to eight players and takes about fourteen minutes.

What about the real game?

If you own a physical copy, a video call works with it too. One person reads the card, others answer aloud and a shared note keeps score. It is slower than a screen game, but it is lovely for a family that likes the feel of the cards.

Why did the idea spread so quickly?

A quiz game needs no skill with dice or strategy, only knowledge and nerve. Anyone can join, and the questions create stories. People remember the answer they got wrong for years. That is why a quiz travels so easily from a box to a call, and why online board games and party games online keep a quiz among their first choices.

What would you change today?

The categories are the part that ages strongest. The specific questions date quickly, so a modern quiz refreshes them often and checks each answer against a source. That habit, a named source for every answer, is the quiet improvement of the online era.

A note on the facts

Dates and sales come from one reference article and may be updated. If you use them in a talk, say that they are as recorded there.

A closing line

An idea written down in Montreal in December 1979 turned into a habit in millions of homes. A good question still does the same work.
