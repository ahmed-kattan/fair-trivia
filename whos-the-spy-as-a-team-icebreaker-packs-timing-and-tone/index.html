Who's the Spy as a team icebreaker: packs, timing and tone

Ahmed Moaz · 14 November 2026

Teams that work apart need an excuse to talk. Who's the Spy gives one, and takes seven minutes. This is how I would run it for colleagues.

Choose packs that fit the room

The host picks the word packs. For colleagues, school and office, tech and gadgets and hobbies are comfortable. They do not require personal knowledge. Avoid anything that could embarrass someone.

Pick the mode

If the team is remote, use Online, where clues are typed. If everyone is in the same room, use Same room, with phones and clues said aloud. For a large offsite, One phone takes up to twelve.

Keep it short

The middle Who's the Spy game took 7.1 minutes in Boardit's table log, for finished games between 13 September and 30 October 2026. Plan for two or three rounds and a debrief.

A sample five-minute opener

  1. Join, 1 minute. A guest needs only a name.
  2. One rule line, 30 seconds. Everyone has the word except the spy, who knows the category.
  3. Round one, 3 minutes.
  4. Vote and reveal, 30 seconds.

Make it safe

  1. Say it is a game of bluffing. Lying is the point.
  2. Do not keep a ranked score. Let it be light.
  3. Let the spy be anyone. Managers should take a turn too.

Why it works

It makes people talk to each other in short, low-stakes bursts. A spy must listen, and civilians must explain themselves. Those are small versions of useful skills.

What not to do

Do not play it as a reward for a hard week with a long game. Do not force the quiet to speak first. Do not repeat the same pack on consecutive weeks. No word repeats at one table, but a fresh pack keeps interest.

Chat

Online, there are controls to hide a player's messages, report them or turn chat off.

A jobs pack for work

For colleagues the Jobs pack has 23 pairs, 46 words, and it reads as work without being about anyone's actual job. It is a good first pack for an icebreaker.

The icebreaker in a line

Seven minutes, a light pack and a manager who gets caught.
